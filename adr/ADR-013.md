# ADR-013: Warrant, Epistemic Status, and Execution Disposition

**Status:** Proposed
**Work order:** OIC-WO-002
**Architecture authority:** GPT-5.6 Thinking. **Final design authority:** Arkadiy Miteiko / Veraxis.
**Scope:** architecture, proposed schemas, and conformance fixtures only. No ZTL call, no VEIP call, no admission, no envelope generation, no Rego, no runtime execution. The semantic implementation gate remains **BLOCKED**.

---

## 1. Problem

`schemas/draft/runtime-evaluation.schema.json` records the outcome of an evaluation in a
single field:

```json
"verdict": { "enum": ["ALLOW", "DENY", "CANNOT"] }
```

One field is being asked to carry three different things at once:

1. what the admitted grounds establish about the claim;
2. what the runtime operationally did;
3. why it did it.

Those three come apart, and the place they come apart is exactly where the system is most
dangerous. Two examples that are not hypothetical:

**The measured ZTL case.** The dossier reports, against the pinned kernel:

```
judge("p & q", {"p": "T", "q": "Z"})
  -> verdict='F', grade='until-verification', disposition='OPEN', unverified=['q']
```

The raw verdict is `F` while the disposition is `OPEN`. An adapter that reads `verdict`
turns *"q has not been verified"* into *"the claim is false"*. That is a direct violation
of **I-03** (unknown never serialises as grounded false), and it will not surface in
ordinary testing, because it needs a genuinely undetermined case to reach the runtime.

**The `on_unknown = deny` case.** `schemas/draft/control-envelope.schema.json` already
permits `on_unknown: "deny"`. That is a reasonable *operational* posture — fail closed when
you do not know. But if the resulting record says `verdict: "DENY"` and nothing else, then
a fail-closed block and a grounds-established prohibition are byte-identical downstream.
Any consumer — an appeal, an audit, a change-impact analysis, a VEIP reliance record —
will read the second when only the first happened.

The failure mode in both cases is the same: **absence of verification is recorded as
established negation.** Once that record exists it propagates, and nothing downstream can
recover the distinction, because the distinguishing information was never written down.

## 2. Decision

Record the three things in three fields, and never let one stand in for another.

### 2.1 Terminology

**Ground.** An admitted proposition supplied *to* the kernel. Grounds arrive from OIC's
admission plane. The kernel does not read documents and does not decide what is admitted.

**Warrant artifact.** A recomputable record of what a logic kernel established over a
supplied ground set, bound to a pinned kernel, an input hash, a ground-set hash, and a
ground epoch. A warrant is *evidence*, not an assertion: `recomputation_reference` is
required so it can be re-derived rather than trusted.

**Warranty grade.** How durable the result is under further verification:
`hereditary` ⟹ `sound` ⟹ (weakest) `until-verification`. `hereditary` is invariant under
every admissible monotone refinement of the *same* formula and marking.

**Epistemic status.** What the admitted grounds establish about the claim.

**Execution disposition.** What the runtime did.

**Decision basis.** Why the runtime did that.

### 2.2 The three dimensions

**A — `epistemic_status`**

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ESTABLISHED` | The grounds establish the claim **independently of any unresolved ground**, under the profile's hereditary contract. |
| `CONDITIONALLY_SUPPORTED` | The current admitted marking supports the claim, but the positive result **depends on one or more unverified grounds** and is not invariant under admissible refinement. |
| `REFUTED` | The grounds establish its negation. |
| `UNRESOLVED` | The grounds do not settle it, **or it was never validly evaluated**. |
| `CONTRADICTED` | The admitted ground set is inconsistent. |

Mapping from the `ztl-v0.1` profile: `EARNED` → `ESTABLISHED`, `ON CREDIT` →
`CONDITIONALLY_SUPPORTED`, `OPEN` → `UNRESOLVED`, `REFUTED` → `REFUTED`, contradictory
grounds → `CONTRADICTED`.

`UNRESOLVED` is not a weak `REFUTED`. `CONTRADICTED` is not `REFUTED` either: inconsistent
grounds establish nothing about the claim, they establish something about the grounds.

`CONTRADICTED` is an **OIC-plane** classification, not a fifth ZTL disposition. The ZTL
marking model assigns one `T`/`F`/`Z` value per atom and cannot represent two conflicting
admitted values for the same atom, so the kernel never reports this condition — OIC detects
it when admission supplies conflicting grounds. Distinguish it from a contradictory
*formula*, which ZTL simply `REFUTES`.

**`ESTABLISHED` is reserved** for positive results independent of unresolved grounds. It is
not a synonym for "the verdict was T". `ON CREDIT` returns `T` and **must never serialise as
`ESTABLISHED`** — doing so authorises action on an unverified link, which is the failure a
zero-trust warrant exists to expose. It must equally never collapse to `UNRESOLVED` merely
because grounds are unverified: the result genuinely holds in the present marking, and
discarding it would over-block.

**Permission to act on conditional support is an admitted control decision, not a stronger
epistemic claim.** The control grants it through `unverified_ground_policy`; the epistemic
status is unchanged when it does.

**B — `execution_disposition`**

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ALLOW` | The action may proceed. |
| `BLOCK` | The action is stopped. |
| `ESCALATE` | Routed to a human or higher authority. |
| `ADVISORY` | Recorded, does not gate the action. |

**C — `decision_basis`**

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SUBSTANTIVE` | The admitted grounds determine a positive, negative, or contradictory epistemic result. |
| `PRECAUTIONARY` | The grounds remain unresolved and the runtime fails closed. |
| `PROCEDURAL` | The claim could not be validly evaluated: an artifact, binding, version, availability, or integrity precondition failed. |
| `CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` | The claim **was** validly evaluated, but the admitted control requires a particular assurance grade, human role, escalation path, or non-automatic decision mode. |

These answer *"could this have gone differently, and who would change it?"*

`PRECAUTIONARY` — the logic ran and ground is genuinely missing. Verify the ground and
re-run. `PROCEDURAL` — the logic never ran, or ran against the wrong world. Fix the
plumbing and re-run. `CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` — everything worked and the institution
reserved the decision. Nothing is broken; a person or a policy change is required.

`CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` was added because the alternative was to report an institutional
policy as either a defect or a doubt. When a control says *"a human decides this"*,
recording `PROCEDURAL` sends an engineer to fix a pipeline that is not broken, and
recording `PRECAUTIONARY` suggests the grounds were lacking when they were not.

It applies to: `EARNED` or `ON CREDIT` where the observed grade is below the control's
declared minimum; `decision_mode` of `human_judgment`, `escalation_only`, or
`non_automatable`; `decision_mode` of `advisory` or `evidence_only`; and any other explicit
envelope rule that overrides an otherwise established result **without invalidating the
warrant**.

**`CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` never rewrites `epistemic_status`.** An escalated `ESTABLISHED`
stays `ESTABLISHED`, and the schema enforces that a decision carrying this basis also
carries a control-policy reason code: `OIC-W-0016`, `OIC-D-0002`, `OIC-D-0003`, `OIC-D-0005`, or `OIC-D-0006`.

### 2.3 The load-bearing invariants

**W-1.** ZTL `disposition = OPEN`, **whatever the raw verdict**, maps to
`epistemic_status = UNRESOLVED`. It MUST NEVER map to `REFUTED`.

**W-2.** `raw_verdict` is recorded for replay only and MUST NOT drive any decision.

**W-3.** A `PRECAUTIONARY`, `PROCEDURAL`, or `CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` outcome MUST NOT be
serialised, reported, or reused as an established negative fact or a grounded prohibition.
Precisely:

* `SUBSTANTIVE` + `ESTABLISHED` is a positive finding about the admitted grounds;
* `SUBSTANTIVE` + `REFUTED` is a negative finding about the admitted grounds;
* `SUBSTANTIVE` + `CONTRADICTED` is a finding that the admitted ground set is inconsistent
  — **not** a refutation of the proposition;
* `PRECAUTIONARY`, `PROCEDURAL`, and `CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` are **not** findings that the
  proposition is false.

`OIC-W-0013` and `OIC-W-0014` are the only substantive BLOCK reason codes. `OIC-D-0001` is
the substantive positive-decision code.

**W-4.** `execution_disposition = ALLOW` has exactly **two** routes. Both require every
common condition:

1. `warrant_state = USABLE`;
2. non-null `warrant_artifact_id`;
3. kernel profile and canonicalization profile bindings match;
4. formula hash matches, compared byte-for-byte after the profiles match;
5. epoch, source-version, admission-version, and ground-set-hash all match;
6. no expiry and no revocation in force;
7. authority, admission, evidence, and version checks **outside** the kernel pass;
8. `decision_mode` permits automatic execution.

**Route A — the `EARNED` / `hereditary` route**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `epistemic_status` | `ESTABLISHED` |
| disposition source | `EARNED` |
| observed grade | `hereditary` |
| `missing_ground_ids` | any — informational when present |
| `decision_basis` | `SUBSTANTIVE` |
| reason codes | `OIC-D-0001` |

**Route B — the `ON CREDIT` / `sound` route**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `epistemic_status` | `CONDITIONALLY_SUPPORTED` |
| disposition source | `ON CREDIT` |
| observed grade | `sound` |
| required grade | permits `sound` |
| `unverified_ground_policy` | `allow_with_disclosure` |
| `missing_ground_ids` | non-empty |
| `decision_basis` | `CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` |
| reason codes | `OIC-D-0005` and `OIC-W-0015` |
| subscription | non-null reference, ground ids equal to `missing_ground_ids`, all five triggers |

Failing any common condition, or matching neither route, yields `BLOCK` or `ESCALATE`,
never `ALLOW`. Condition 7 is not decorative: ZTL does not check authority or admission and
says so plainly; a warrant is necessary for `ALLOW` and nowhere near sufficient.

**W-5.** Whenever the kernel reports unverified grounds, OIC preserves them.

Under `ztl-v0.1`:

- **`EARNED`** may carry unverified grounds that are **irrelevant to this conclusion**
  because the result is `hereditary`;
- **`ON CREDIT`** carries unverified grounds **on which the current positive result
  depends**;
- **`OPEN`** carries unverified grounds **whose resolution is needed**.

One field, three meanings. Measured: `EARNED` carries a non-empty list in 61 of 294 census
cases, and 138 refinements over those atoms moved the verdict 0 times. A contract that does
not distinguish the three roles will get one of them wrong — either treating an
`EARNED` result as weaker than it is, or treating an `ON CREDIT` result as stronger.

**W-6.** `on_unknown = deny` produces `execution_disposition = BLOCK` with
`decision_basis` of `PRECAUTIONARY` or `PROCEDURAL`, **never** `SUBSTANTIVE`.

**W-7.** Unknown grade values (a kernel returning a grade OIC does not know) are
`UNSUPPORTED_GRADE` → `UNRESOLVED` / `PROCEDURAL`. Never optimistic, never guessed.

**W-8.** Evaluation runs in **three ordered stages**: (1) classification, (2) warrant
policy — grade sufficiency first, then the unverified-ground policy — and (3) decision-mode
overlay, applied last. Stages 2 and 3 may change `execution_disposition`, `decision_basis`,
and policy reason codes. Neither MUST change `epistemic_status`. Every matched policy
reason code is **retained** even when a later stage changes the final execution
disposition, and `applied_control_overlay_ids` records which rules fired, in deterministic
order, without duplicates. Every overlay in the canonical
mapping declares `epistemic_effect = PRESERVE`, and the mapping is split into
classification rows and overlays so that an overlay has no field in which to assert a
status of its own. Under the earlier flat table, a `decision_mode` row carried its own
`epistemic_status`, which meant `REFUTED` + `human_judgment` recorded an *established
positive*.

**W-12.** `dependency_ids` is every **verified** atom in the kernel-evaluated formula, and
is disjoint from `unverified_ground_ids` by construction. **Minimality is not claimed.**
Measured: in 38 of 180 census cases with two or more verified grounds, withdrawing a *pair*
moves the disposition although neither member does alone. `(p | q) & r` with `{p:T, q:T,
r:T}` is `EARNED`; withdrawing `p` alone or `q` alone stays `EARNED`; withdrawing both
yields `OPEN`. A set minimised by single-ground probing omits both, so a revocation of both
propagates to nothing and a warrant claiming `ESTABLISHED` outlives the grounds that
established it. The over-approximation fails safe; minimisation fails open.

**W-13.** `formula_hash` is computed over the **kernel-rendered** formula returned by
`ztljudge.judge`, never over the caller's original string. Caller input `p | q` is rendered
`(p ∨ q)`, and that rendering is what is hashed.

**W-14.** `output_hash` covers the semantic output projection — kernel-rendered formula,
disposition, raw verdict, warranty grade, unverified identifiers — and **excludes `why`**,
which is presentational and may change without a profile version bump.

**W-15.** A `CONDITIONALLY_SUPPORTED` `ALLOW` requires a deterministic recomputation
**subscription**: a non-null reference, coverage equal to `missing_ground_ids`, and all five
triggers (`ground_verified`, `ground_expired`, `ground_revoked`, `ground_corrected`,
`relevant_epoch_changed`). Acting on a result that rides an unverified link is only
defensible if the system will be told when that link moves. A missing or incomplete
subscription is `OIC-W-0027` and cannot `ALLOW`. A subscription is plumbing and is **never**
evidence of stronger epistemic support.

**W-9.** `CONDITIONALLY_SUPPORTED` never carries `SUBSTANTIVE`, never omits
`missing_ground_ids`, never uses `on_unknown_applied`, is never converted to `ESTABLISHED`,
and never reaches `ALLOW` on grade `until-verification`.

**W-10.** `ALLOW` has exactly two positive shapes:

1. `ESTABLISHED` + `SUBSTANTIVE` — the `EARNED`/`hereditary` route;
2. `CONDITIONALLY_SUPPORTED` + `CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` — the `ON CREDIT`/`sound` route,
   requiring `unverified_ground_policy = allow_with_disclosure`, `OIC-D-0005`, and
   non-empty missing grounds preserved on the record.

A minimum warranty grade alone is **not** authorisation to act on unverified grounds. The
grade says how durable the result is; the policy says whether the institution accepts a
result that rides an unverified link.

**W-11.** Downstream VEIP records **must preserve** the conditional status, the missing
grounds, the observed grade, and the applied policy. A consumer that stores only `ALLOW`
has destroyed the only evidence that the permission was conditional.

### 2.4 Failure semantics

Canonical table in
[`docs/contracts/ZTL-OCE-MAPPING-v0.1.json`](../docs/contracts/ZTL-OCE-MAPPING-v0.1.json).
Every row has a golden fixture. Summary of the required posture:

| Condition | Epistemic | Disposition | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warrant absent, kernel unavailable, malformed, unverifiable | `UNRESOLVED` | BLOCK / ESCALATE | `PROCEDURAL` |
| `warrant_requirement` missing or invalid | `UNRESOLVED` | BLOCK / ESCALATE | `PROCEDURAL` |
| Stale, expired, not-yet-valid warrant | `UNRESOLVED` | BLOCK / ESCALATE | `PROCEDURAL` |
| Epoch, source-version, admission-version, formula, or profile mismatch | `UNRESOLVED` | BLOCK / ESCALATE | `PROCEDURAL` |
| Missing source anchors or admission IDs | `UNRESOLVED` | BLOCK / ESCALATE | `PROCEDURAL` |
| Unsupported grade vocabulary | `UNRESOLVED` | BLOCK / ESCALATE | `PROCEDURAL` |
| Unsupported result combination | `UNRESOLVED` | BLOCK / ESCALATE | `PROCEDURAL` |
| Explicit `not_required` or `not_applicable` | `UNRESOLVED` | BLOCK / ESCALATE | `PROCEDURAL` |
| Revoked or expired ground | `UNRESOLVED` | BLOCK / ESCALATE | `PRECAUTIONARY` |
| `OPEN` (raw `F` or `Z`) | `UNRESOLVED` | BLOCK / ESCALATE | `PRECAUTIONARY` |
| `REFUTED` | `REFUTED` | BLOCK | `SUBSTANTIVE` |
| Contradictory grounds (OIC-plane) | `CONTRADICTED` | BLOCK | `SUBSTANTIVE` |
| `EARNED` + `hereditary`, all checks pass | `ESTABLISHED` | ALLOW | `SUBSTANTIVE` |
| `ON CREDIT` + `sound` + `allow_with_disclosure` | `CONDITIONALLY_SUPPORTED` | ALLOW | `CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` |
| `ON CREDIT` + `sound` + `forbid` | `CONDITIONALLY_SUPPORTED` | BLOCK | `CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` |
| `ON CREDIT` + `sound` + `escalate` | `CONDITIONALLY_SUPPORTED` | ESCALATE | `CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` |
| `ON CREDIT` + `until-verification` | `CONDITIONALLY_SUPPORTED` | BLOCK / ESCALATE | `PRECAUTIONARY` |
| Grade below the control's declared minimum | **classification preserved** | BLOCK / ESCALATE | `CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` |
| `decision_mode` reserves or advises | **prior status preserved** | ESCALATE / ADVISORY | `CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` |
| Unverified grounds non-empty | recorded, never suppressed | unchanged | unchanged |

Two rows state a **preservation** rule rather than a value, and that is deliberate.

*Grade insufficiency* preserves whatever the kernel's disposition classified. Applied to
`ON CREDIT` it yields `CONDITIONALLY_SUPPORTED`; applied to `EARNED` it would yield
`ESTABLISHED`. It does **not** always produce `ESTABLISHED`, and an earlier draft of this
table said it did.

*Decision-mode overlays* preserve the prior epistemic status without exception:
`REFUTED` + `human_judgment` is `REFUTED` / `ESCALATE`, and `CONTRADICTED` +
`human_judgment` is `CONTRADICTED` / `ESCALATE`.

Whether BLOCK or ESCALATE applies is chosen by `on_unknown` on the failure paths, by
`on_insufficient_grade` for grade insufficiency, and by `unverified_ground_policy` for
conditional support. None of those choices changes the epistemic status.

`ON CREDIT` + `until-verification` is never an automatic `ALLOW` in v0.1. **The contract
does not model sufficient risk, reversibility, compensation, or recovery controls to permit
automatic consequential `ALLOW` on a result that rides an atom which can flip**, and it has
no way to express what happens to an action already taken when it does.

### 2.5 Missing grounds: two levels

`missing_ground_ids` — stable atom or proposition identifiers, exactly as the kernel emits
them. Level 1.

`missing_ground_anchors` — human-review references linking each missing proposition to
source document, source node or span, admitted normative unit, and expected evidence type.
Level 2.

The kernel emits level 1 only. Enrichment to level 2 is the OIC adapter's job, when the
adapter is eventually authorised. **The kernel is never asked to interpret source
documents** — that would breach the ZTL boundary (ADR-009) in the direction most likely to
look helpful. An empty `missing_ground_anchors` means enrichment has not run; it does not
mean there are no missing grounds. This answers the `MissingGround` granularity question
the ZTL side raised.

## 3. ZTL boundary

### 3.1 The disposition correction

ZTL dossier v0.1 listed **three** dispositions. The kernel has **four**. `ON CREDIT` was
omitted and is the one that matters most for an enforcement contract: a `T` verdict that is
true **only while an unverified atom holds**, and that can die when that atom resolves.

| Disposition | Grade | Raw | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EARNED` | `hereditary` | `T` | Grounded outright; unverified atoms irrelevant |
| `REFUTED` | `hereditary` | `F` | False regardless of the marks |
| **`ON CREDIT`** | `sound` / `until-verification` | `T` | True only on credit |
| `OPEN` | `until-verification` | `F` / `Z` | Not established; a mark matters |

Mapped as `EARNED`, `ON CREDIT` authorises action on an unverified link. Mapped as `OPEN`
it over-blocks a verdict that under `sound` never lies about the present marking. It
therefore gets its own rows, gated by the control's declared minimum grade.

Three combinations this ADR originally specified are **measured NOT_REACHABLE**:
`EARNED`+`sound`, `EARNED`+`until-verification`, and `OPEN` with raw `T`. They are retained
as defensive fail-closed rows rather than deleted, so an adapter meeting one — from a
substitute kernel, or from a future ZTL change — fails closed instead of falling through.

### 3.2 Frozen profile

The kernel's interface and vocabulary are pinned in
[`docs/contracts/kernel-profiles/ztl-v0.1.json`](../docs/contracts/kernel-profiles/ztl-v0.1.json),
and every `WarrantArtifact` declares the `kernel_profile_id` it was produced under. Five
hazards are recorded there, the sharpest being **ZTL-H-001**: `zverify.grade` expects `M`
for a mark while `judge()` accepts `Z`, and passing `Z` to `grade` returns a wrong grade
**silently** — `hereditary` where the truth is `until-verification`. That is a silent
upgrade of a conditional result to the strongest warranty, so `zverify.grade` is a
**prohibited entrypoint** and OIC consumes `judge()` only.

### 3.3 Boundary

ZTL **ends** with: logical disposition; warranty grade; formula and dependency
information; verified and unverified grounds; epoch and freshness information;
recomputation evidence.

ZTL does **not**: authenticate a source; determine authority, delegation, or jurisdiction;
interpret prose; create admission; decide ALLOW/BLOCK/ESCALATE; create a VEIP lifecycle
record.

OIC consumes `disposition`, `warranty_grade`, and `unverified` as the operational triple.
`raw_verdict` is an internal kernel detail recorded for replay (W-2).

`hereditary` is absorbing under monotone refinement **and only under it**. It does not
waive re-checking on expiry, revocation, correction, source invalidation, schema change,
formula change, semantic-version change, or institutional admissibility. Treating it as
"settled forever" is, in the ZTL side's own words, the most attractive wrong reading of
the warranty ladder, and W-4 conditions 4–8 exist to prevent it.

Expiry is **scoped**, never global: `valid_until` is per-artifact and
`revocation_references` are per-ground. Unrestricted expiry makes every warranty invariant
trivially true, which would turn the whole apparatus into decoration.

### 4.2 Provisional chronology

1. VEIP receives or records the consequential **`ActionProposal`**.
2. The proposal is in the VEIP **Proposed** state.
3. **OIC** evaluates the proposal against the admitted control, warrant, and evidence.
4. **OIC** returns a `RuntimeDecision`.
5. VEIP records the `RuntimeDecision` as **evidence governing whether and how the proposal
   may transition**.
6. VEIP owns execution, recording, reliance, revocation propagation, and correction.

`RuntimeDecision` is therefore:

- the **OIC evaluation output**;
- **evidence inside** the VEIP lifecycle;
- **not** itself a VEIP lifecycle record;
- **not** the first VEIP artifact.

The distinction matters because a system that treats an evaluation output as a lifecycle
record has no place to put the proposal that preceded it, and will invent one — usually by
back-dating the decision into the role of the request.

### 4.3 What the future handoff must bind

Not decided here. The eventual handoff must bind **at least**:

- `action_proposal_id`;
- `envelope_id` and envelope hash;
- `runtime_decision_id` and decision hash;
- `warrant_artifact_id` and warrant hash, where a warrant applies;
- evaluation input hash;
- authority and admission versions.

**Final VEIP transition names and event schemas are deliberately not decided in this PR.**
Arkadiy Miteiko is interim VEIP contract owner (OIC-OWNER-DECISION-002 §5); the schema is
deferred to OIC-GC-004.

### 4.4 The two directions that must not cross

**ZTL must not create a VEIP lifecycle record.** **VEIP must not reinterpret the ZTL
formula.** A VEIP implementation that re-derives a decision from `formula` rather than
consuming `execution_disposition` has crossed back over the boundary, and a kernel that
emits a lifecycle event has crossed the other way.

## 5. Invariant preservation

| Invariant | How this contract preserves it |
|---|---|
| **I-02** no enforcement artifact from a pre-`admitted` state | `admission_ids` required on the warrant; missing admission IDs is a blocking `PROCEDURAL` failure |
| **I-03** unknown never serialises as grounded false | W-1, W-2, W-3, W-6; `UNRESOLVED` is a distinct value and `raw_verdict` is inert |
| **I-04** missing authority/admission/evidence/version/grounds never silently yields ALLOW | W-4, all eight conditions; every failure path lands on BLOCK or ESCALATE |
| **I-05** extraction confidence never substitutes for admission | no confidence field exists anywhere in either proposed schema |
| **I-07** executable artifacts bind versions | `kernel_commit`, `ground_epoch`, `ground_set_hash`, `authority_version`, `admission_version`, `source_version_set_hash` |
| **I-13** revoked/expired authority cannot yield a current allow | revoked and expired grounds are blocking; `valid_until` and `revocation_references` are required fields |
| **I-14** changes create new versions, never silent mutation | epoch and ground-set hash mismatch force recomputation rather than reuse |
| **I-15** every verdict replayable | `recomputation_reference`, `input_hash`, `output_hash`, `replay_reference` |

## 6. Compatibility impact and migration

**Nothing in `schemas/draft/` is modified by this ADR.** The proposals live in
`schemas/proposed/` and are inert until an owner decision admits them. `STATUS.md` is
unchanged and the semantic gate remains BLOCKED.

Three migrations are *proposed*, not applied:

1. **`runtime-evaluation.schema.json` → `runtime-decision.schema.json`.** The single
   `verdict` field is replaced by the three dimensions. The mapping from the old field is
   lossy in one direction only, and that is the point: `DENY` cannot be reconstructed into
   `REFUTED`-versus-fail-closed, because the old schema never recorded which it was. Any
   migration must therefore treat historical `DENY` records as **indeterminate**, not as
   `SUBSTANTIVE`. Back-filling them would manufacture evidence.
2. **`control-envelope.schema.json` needs a `warrant_requirement` object.** No field
   expresses "this control requires a `hereditary` warrant under kernel profile X" today,
   so the permitted-versus-insufficient grade distinction cannot be represented. The
   proposed shape is [`warrant-requirement.schema.json`](../schemas/proposed/warrant-requirement.schema.json),
   with **no default mode**: a control that fails to declare one is a contract error rather
   than a permissive case. An earlier draft proposed a bare `minimum_warranty_grade` field
   defaulting to `hereditary`; that was rejected because a defaulted warrant requirement is
   indistinguishable from one nobody thought about. **Not applied here** — `schemas/draft`
   is Class B and out of scope for this work order.
3. **`on_unknown` gains a documented reading rather than a new value.** `deny` stays, but
   the contract fixes it as an operational fail-closed block only (W-6). No schema change
   is needed; the change is that a conforming implementation must now also record
   `decision_basis`, which makes the distinction visible.

## 7. Rejected alternatives

**Keep one `verdict` field and add a reason code.** Rejected: reason codes are advisory in
practice and get dropped by consumers, while the enum survives. The distinction between
"unverified" and "false" is too load-bearing to carry in an optional-feeling field. It
also leaves `DENY` as the literal serialised value for a merely unknown state, which is
what I-03 forbids.

**Add `UNKNOWN` to the existing verdict enum.** Rejected: it still conflates epistemic
state with execution. `UNKNOWN` would have to mean both "we do not know" and "therefore we
blocked", and a control that escalates on unknown could not be distinguished from one that
blocks.

**Use ZTL's `T`/`F`/`Z` directly as the OIC epistemic status.** Rejected on the ZTL side's
own advice. `Z` is a mark on verification status rather than a third truth value, compound
formulas collapse it, and the raw verdict can be `F` while the disposition is `OPEN`.
Adopting the raw marking would import exactly the confusion W-1 exists to prevent.

**Let the kernel emit clause-level missing-ground anchors.** Rejected: it would require
the kernel to interpret source documents, breaching ADR-009. Two levels, with enrichment
on the OIC side, keeps the boundary intact.

**Treat `hereditary` as permanently settled and skip revalidation.** Rejected as measured
error: `hereditary` is absorbing under monotone refinement only, and says nothing about
expiry, revocation, or correction.

**Define expiry globally.** Rejected as measured error: from any marking `{expire, verify}`
reaches every marking, so a property invariant under refinement *and* arbitrary expiry is
constant — a frame that cannot fail, and a test that proves nothing.

## 9. Owner decisions incorporated

Recorded as received from Arkadiy Miteiko / Veraxis, final product and design authority.

### 10.1 Historical legacy DENY records

Historical records containing only `ALLOW` / `DENY` / `CANNOT` are **immutable** and
classified **`LEGACY_UNDECOMPOSED`**.

They **must not** be back-filled as `REFUTED`, `PRECAUTIONARY`, `PROCEDURAL`, or
`CONTROL_REQUIREMENT` without the original evidence needed to reconstruct that distinction.
The old schema never recorded which kind of `DENY` it was, so any back-fill would be
manufactured evidence rather than migration.

A consumer requiring current epistemic meaning must **reevaluate the case under this
contract**. A legacy record answers what happened, not what is true now.

### 10.2 Expiry authority

- Validity derives from admitted **source, authority, admission, and evidence** records.
- An admission may **narrow** but may **not extend** upstream validity.
- Revocation requires an **authorized issuer**, a **revocation authority**, or an **admitted
  correction record**.
- The effective boundary is the **earliest active applicable boundary**.
- The logic kernel **does not create** validity or revocation authority. It computes the
  consequences of expiry; it does not schedule or authorise it.
- Runtime time evidence will be recorded **through the VEIP boundary**.

This is why `ground_epoch` carries an `authority_id` and `time_binding` names its clock:
both encode who was entitled to move the boundary.

### 10.3 VEIP handoff

`RuntimeDecision` alone is **not** the complete VEIP handoff. It must be bound to the
pre-existing `ActionProposal` and to the exact envelope, warrant, inputs, authority, and
admission versions. See §4.3 for the minimum binding set.
