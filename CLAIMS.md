# Claims Register

## Permitted design statements at bootstrap

- OIC is designed to convert heterogeneous institutional documents into source-anchored candidate controls.
- OIC separates extracted or inferred meaning from institutionally admitted meaning.
- OIC defines a target-independent Open Control Envelope as its proposed enforcement boundary.
- OIC is designed to preserve unresolved ambiguity and explicit CANNOT outcomes.
- The open repository and hosted Lab are required to use the same semantic compiler core.

These are architecture statements, not measured quality claims.

## Claims requiring frozen evidence

- Source-supported executable-field percentage.
- Normative field accuracy.
- Authority reconstruction accuracy.
- Ambiguity recall and false-resolution rate.
- Runtime behavioral conformance.
- Change-impact recall.
- Practitioner review-time reduction.
- Comparative outperformance against a named baseline.

## Forbidden at current status

- OIC guarantees legal or regulatory compliance.
- OIC eliminates legal, policy, compliance, audit, or authorized human review.
- OIC autonomously determines correct institutional meaning.
- OIC is enterprise-ready.
- OIC is universally the first or best institutional compiler.
- OIC outperforms all rules-as-code, GRC, policy, or agent-governance systems.
- ZTL proves source authority or institutional legitimacy.
- VEIP creates policy meaning or authority.
- A compiled control is valid merely because it passes tests.
